After two stressful PhD thesis submissions, one nerve-racking PhD defense and a stack of moving boxes, our time in the U.S. was finally coming to an end. Before we moved to Germany, there was one item left on my bucket list: Yosemite. My husband had already been once, so this time he got to play tour guide.

The timing couldn't have been better. My mother-in-law had flown in for my husband's graduation, and we wanted to show her what we would miss most about this country: its unbelievable nature! So at the start of summer, just as the waterfalls were at their fullest, we packed the car for one last American road trip.

We planned a quick getaway to [Yosemite National Park](https://www.nps.gov/yose/index.htm), about a four-to-five-hour drive from San Jose. The drive itself was peaceful in the most literal sense: it took us through rural California, with long stretches of road where we saw no one, not a single car. Just us and open land stretching as far as the eye could see. It was beautiful, and it only got better once we entered the park, where we caught our first glimpse of the valley and an intimidating view of the mighty [El Capitan](https://www.yosemite.com/yosemite-mariposa-county-region/yosemite-valley/el-capitan/).

Since this was my husband's second visit to Yosemite, he knew exactly what to show us first to make an impression. And it did! We drove through the Wawona Tunnel to the famous [Tunnel View](https://www.yosemite.com/things-to-do/leisure-activities/tunnel-view/). The whole valley lay in front of us, and one thing was certain: this was nature showing off! Towering granite mountains of all different shapes, their walls full of cracks and crevices, waterfalls tumbling down the cliffs, and all of it resting on a thick blanket of pine and cedar trees: each sight more impressive than the last!

Yosemite is famous for its waterfalls, and we hoped to see some of them at their fullest. See the waterfall in the Tunnel View photo? That's where we headed next. [Bridalveil Fall](https://www.nps.gov/places/000/bridalveil-fall.htm) is aptly named: like a bride's veil, it was a little shy. But it was beautiful. Happy with our introduction to Yosemite, we retired for the night to our lovely Airbnb cottage.

We started the next day with something we won't miss about the U.S.: hunting for a parking spot. After signing up for the NPS traffic and parking alerts, we found one fairly quickly. We spent the rest of the day using Yosemite's shuttle buses and walking from the stops to the other sights. 

First up was a short hike to [Yosemite Falls](https://www.yosemite.com/things-to-do/leisure-activities/yosemite-falls/). The trail to Upper Yosemite Fall is an adventure of its own, so we settled for a quick hike to the base of Lower Yosemite Fall. The spray was refreshing on a hot day, and it was perhaps the most authentic California summer experience I've had. Despite the sunscreen, the tan from this trip stayed with me for two or three months.

Afterwards, we explored a few more trails. My favorite one was Cook's Meadow, which was a leisurely walk with a panoramic view of the entire valley. From Cook's Meadow you can see Yosemite Falls on one side and, on the other, the unmistakable [Half Dome](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/halfdome.htm): a granite dome rising about 1,450 meters (4,700 feet) above the valley floor, looking as if someone had sliced it clean down the middle. The hike to its top is one of the most famous in the country, ending with a climb up steel cables, and you need to win a permit in a lottery just to attempt it. We were happy to admire it from the meadow.

For our final day in Yosemite, we went to see the giant sequoias at the [Mariposa Grove](https://www.nps.gov/yose/planyourvisit/mg.htm). We wandered along the trails through the grove, and I remember posing in front of the [Fallen Monarch](https://redwoodsinyosemite.com/fallen-monarch-in-the-mariposa-grove/) and feeling absolutely tiny. Even lying on its side, it towered over us. The walk through the grove also took us back to our trip to Redwood National Park [last year](https://mugdhakhedkar.com/Redwood-Coast/).

Yosemite was the perfect way to say goodbye to the U.S., and to show our family the nature we have come to love here. On all our travels around this country, we met the kindest people: strangers who smiled at us on the street, chatted with us on trails and offered to take our photos. That warmth is something I might actually miss. Maybe in a parallel universe, or in a future with blue seas and bluer skies, we will find our way back, to visit or maybe even to stay. Until then, I am eternally grateful for the memories.
